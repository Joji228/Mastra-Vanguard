# Mastra Vanguard audit — October 11, 2026

Reviewed version **0.11**, checkout **b3b639f**. The original audit added only this report and reproducible diagnostic tooling. The findings below record that pre-fix snapshot.

## Implementation follow-up

The three confirmed bugs have now been fixed. Pointer activation of Sound/Fullscreen and clicking the canvas restore gameplay focus, while keyboard activation of toolbar controls retains accessible focus. Decorative hero/boss/drone pulses, fallback hit flashes and the Manta's hit-opacity effect now respect Reduced Flashing. Late successful artwork updates its failure count and notice automatically, preserves dismissal, and uses an artwork-session guard to avoid changing a later mission's status. Automatic recovery also works alongside manual Retry.

`tools/audit-fixes-test.mjs` adds real-Chrome regressions for all four modes, normal/reduced rendering, all enemy/boss fallbacks, multiple late images, dismissed notices, Retry and stage switching. See [Development & verification](DEVELOPMENT.md) for the optional browser runtime configuration. Version remains **0.11**; assets and combat tuning are unchanged.

Follow-up validation passes: smoke tests, the new audit-fix browser regressions, the full Chrome campaign audit (all three completion paths and 30 restarts), the 768-case menu/HUD audit, presentation checks and whitespace checks. No captured page/console errors occurred in the full campaign audit.

The game is stable in the tested paths. Three issues warrant fixes: gameplay focus after using the Sound button, incomplete Reduced Flashing behavior, and stale artwork-failure notices after slow successful loads. Asset delivery and combat balance are the strongest improvement opportunities. Preserve the shared hero, existing flight tuning and art direction.

## 1. Sound button can leave the player without keyboard control

**Reproduction:** start a stage, click the top-right Sound button, then press a movement or power key. Click the game canvas and try again. Keyboard controls remain blocked while the mission continues. Esc followed by Resume restores them.

In a Chrome probe, D registered and moved the hero before clicking Sound. After clicking Sound, D was rejected and displacement was zero. Clicking the canvas left `document.activeElement.id === 'mute'`, with D still rejected. Pause/resume returned focus to `game` and movement worked again. Velocity was reset before each sample to distinguish new input from flight momentum.

**Cause:** `Input` intentionally ignores gameplay keys from interactive DOM controls (`index.html:751–753`). The Sound click handler (`2450`) retains button focus, and canvas mousedown (`765`) prevents the default focus change without explicitly focusing the canvas. That combination makes a routine toolbar action strand gameplay input.

**Recommendation:** explicitly focus the canvas when the player clicks it. For pointer activation of gameplay utility buttons, consider returning focus to gameplay while a mission is active. Preserve keyboard navigation of menus and controls. Check the Fullscreen button for the same focus path; the exact native-fullscreen behavior was not reproduced by this probe.

Add a browser regression for Sound → canvas click → D/F, plus pause/resume and Training controls. Existing suites pass because they do not cover this sequence.

## 2. Reduced Flashing is inconsistently applied

The existing helpers correctly suppress sprite brightness flashes and several beam/barrier pulses. Other render paths bypass them:

- Astral Devourer's phase ring directly sets alpha to `.42 + .2 * sin(anim * 8)` (`index.html:1613`). With both accessibility settings enabled, the probe recorded ring alpha **0.42, 0.62, 0.22** at three animation times.
- Heliarch Zero's equivalent ring uses `.35 + .18 * sin(anim * 8)` (`1796`), producing **0.35, 0.53, 0.17** under the same settings.
- Warden's missing-art fallback chooses a white body whenever `flash` is nonzero (`1013`), even with Reduced Flashing enabled. The fallback probe recorded the body changing from `#251a31` to `#fff`. Several minion/boss fallbacks use the same direct pattern.

**Recommendation:** use `effectPulse` for decorative brightness and `spriteFlash` or an equivalent helper for every fallback. Keep attack windups and impact feedback readable. Review decorative charging rotations and fallback pulses for Reduced Motion too; that setting already reduces particles and shake but does not freeze every decorative animation.

These findings concern preference consistency. This audit does not measure medical photosensitivity thresholds or certify flashing safety. A useful regression would render normal and failed-art paths at several animation times with each setting enabled, instead of only checking that the preferences persist.

## 3. Late artwork recovery leaves a misleading warning

`readySprite` gives each image eight seconds (`index.html:587`). Once that deadline expires, `requestStartMode` stores the image in `failedArtwork`. A later image load requests a redraw, but does not update that failure list or notice (`567–568`, `2086–2090`).

**Reproduction:** delay the flight sheet's source assignment by 9.5 seconds, then allow a normal successful local image load. The mission started after about **8.1 seconds** with one failed file. When the image arrived and `naturalWidth > 0`, it rendered, but `assetFailures` stayed **1** and the unavailable-artwork notice stayed visible.

**Recommendation:** distinguish pending/slow files from definite errors. Preserve the playable fallback and reconcile the current mission's failure list when a late load/decode succeeds. Guard against an old mission's load event changing a new mission's status. Manual Retry should remain available for true failures.

The reproduction simulates delayed image arrival; it is not a measurement of GitHub Pages download speed.

## 4. Image delivery is the main performance opportunity

Measured primary stage assets, excluding the 0.71 MiB soundtrack and rare fallback images:

| Mode | Required PNGs | Compressed image bytes | Nominal RGBA size |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Stage 1 | 21 | 34.53 MiB | 125.99 MiB |
| Stage 2 | 17 | 30.27 MiB | 102.00 MiB |
| Stage 3 | 18 | 26.44 MiB | 108.00 MiB |
| Training | 29 | 46.92 MiB | 173.99 MiB |

After sequentially visiting all three stages, loaded images represented **203.99 MiB** of nominal RGBA storage, plus **6 MiB** of active tint surfaces. This is width × height × 4 budget accounting, **not** a measured browser memory footprint; browsers can evict decoded resources and have additional overhead. Stage 1 and Stage 2 tint budgets were 12 and 18 MiB respectively. Render targets and animation caches add further storage.

All artwork files, including historical versions, total **88.35 MiB** on disk. Historical files are not all loaded by the game; deleting archives alone would not reduce a player's stage download.

**Recommendation:** keep source artwork and prepare smaller, carefully verified runtime copies. Compare lossless alternate encoding and appropriately sized sheets against the current pixels at actual display size and high DPI. Check transparency, cell boundaries, palm/foot anchors, cropping and grading before adopting changes. Then consider releasing rarely revisited stage resources where the reload cost is acceptable. Training naturally requires a broader asset set.

Existing optimizations already include stage-specific loading, culling, tint caching, bounded particles, a canvas pixel budget and reused aiming composites. The previous PNG optimization is already applied.

Fresh Chrome campaign CPU update/draw-submission medians were approximately **1.1 / 0.4 / 0.5 ms** for Stages 1/2/3. The 48-target/boss stress scene measured **3.6 ms median / 7.9 ms p95** in this run. These are local CPU/submission samples, not GPU completion, sustained FPS, network load time or low-end hardware results. They do not justify a movement or rendering rewrite.

## 5. Balance should be evaluated through counterplay

The old partial-charge advantage and zero-width beam hitbox issues have been fixed. Current release probes show a full-charge advantage:

| Boss | Held click result | Full-charge result | Damage taken with full charges |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Prism Warden | Won in 20.14 s | Won in 15.50 s | 49 |
| Astral Devourer | Player died at 22.98 s; boss had 342 HP | Won in 22.30 s | 46 |
| Heliarch Zero | Won in 32.24 s | Won in 24.75 s | 17 |

These strategies aim automatically and use Guard whenever its cooldown permits. Timings include intro/death sequencing, so they are encounter durations rather than isolated damage throughput.

Additional probes used three seeds and three initial positions per boss: ground, high hover, and right-edge high hover. All **27** full-charge/Guard trials won in normal mode, with damage taken ranging from **17 to 61**. They used no lateral evasive inputs; airborne trials held ascend while flight remained active. Recoil, knockback, energy drain, flight cancellation and regeneration remained real. No damage-free hover result occurred.

**Interpretation:** exact aiming and the current regeneration/Guard/full-charge combination can sustain wins with limited movement. Stage 2 punishes held-click play more strongly, while Stage 3's sampled full-charge strategy was forgiving. This is a reason to evaluate intended difficulty, not proof that the game needs blanket nerfs. Human players cannot reproduce perfect automatic aiming by assumption.

**Recommendation:** playtest with first-time and experienced players, record deaths, encounter duration and which warnings they understand, and compare click, full charge, Nova and flight approaches. If more challenge is desired, give bosses readable opportunities to pressure a charging/hovering player, with fair recovery windows. Preserve flight and the intentionally long beams. Change one interaction at a time before adjusting HP or damage. Optional difficulty choices could support both casual superhero play and a more demanding arcade run.

## Presentation, pacing and convenience

The current menus are legible, the three stages have distinct visual identities, and tested compact HUD layouts retain their controls. Stage 2's purple combat and Stage 3's orange combat share colors with their scenery; maintain the darker warning outlines and consider small contrast improvements where effects overlap the hero. The reviewed still images support readability, but cannot establish reaction-time fairness at Mach speed.

Stage bonuses, a minimized Training panel, physical-layout keys, coyote time, jump buffering, score separation for God Mode and artwork Retry are already implemented. They should not be presented as missing features from the earlier audit.

Useful next additions:

1. **Optional boss retry:** reduce repeating a whole stage while learning a boss. Explicitly mark practice/checkpoint score rules so arcade best scores retain their meaning.
2. **Key rebinding:** include reset-to-default controls and update all displayed hints. A keyboard aiming option could broaden access; gamepad/touch controls are separate larger work.
3. **Guided Training exercises:** short prompts for a timed Guard, full-charge release and Mach landing would teach mechanics better than another static controls list. The existing Training help is a good starting point.
4. **Visual testing for remaining artwork transitions:** inspect Stage 2 attack transitions and source-sheet fragments/cutoffs before generating new assets. The current shared palm/feet registration fixes should be preserved.
